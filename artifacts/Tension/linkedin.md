Everybody stops for a sunset.

Nobody has to tell you it's beautiful. You just know. It's the moment a hard day meets a quiet night, and for a few minutes you get both.

Now picture a tug-of-war.

Each team feels one thing: the other side pulling.
Only the rope feels both.

We've been taught the passion lives at the ends. Pick a team. Dig in. Pull harder.

I think we've got that backwards.

Here's one reason the ends feel so loud. Researchers studied about 2.7 million Facebook and Twitter posts from news outlets and members of Congress. Every extra word aimed at the other side raised the odds of a share by 67%.

Which means this post, with nobody to dunk on, is already starting from behind.

I spent years on one end of the rope. I've stopped pulling. Now I'm holding it.

My rules are simple:
People first.
Facts over tribe.
Institutions earn trust or lose it.

I wrote a longer piece about the middle, and why that's where the passion really belongs. I call it Tension.
