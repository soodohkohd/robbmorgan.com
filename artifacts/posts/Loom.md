Every AI model is obsolete the millisecond it finishes training.

Think about that. The most advanced models on earth ship with a hard cutoff baked into their weights. Everything after that date is a blind spot. The industry's answer? Retrain the whole thing every few months and call it progress. That's not a solution. That's the same mistake on a schedule.

I've been building something different. It's called Loom.

The idea is simple. Stop cramming knowledge into the model. A model should reason, not remember. Reasoning doesn't expire. Facts do. So you don't need a bigger model. You need one that reasons well, and nothing else. A small model already does that. Leave the weights sealed and move the knowledge onto disk, where you can verify it, update it, and let it grow. It learns from itself. It builds on itself.

Three parts. A small model that runs on my own hardware and does the reasoning. A knowledge store that lives in files I own and gets smarter over time. And a harness that uses tools to go find what the model doesn't know, check it against reality, and write it down.

The rule that makes it work: nothing is trusted just because it sounds right. Not the model's memory. Not a confident answer. Not even me. A claim only becomes a fact when something outside the system confirms it. Run the code. Cross-check the sources. Let reality grade it, not a vibe.

The result is a system that catches its own stale knowledge, refuses to store a guess as a fact, and gets sharper the longer it runs.

And it does it while you're talking to it. Not a nightly retrain. Not a batch job that lands next Tuesday. When it hits something it doesn't know, it goes and finds out, checks it, and writes it down before it answers. The next question starts from what it just learned. The model never changes. The system never stops learning.

No provider. No subscription that evaporates when the next model drops. Reasoning I own, knowledge I control, running on hardware in my own room.

Stale models are a design choice. I'm building the alternative.

So here's my challenge to you: tell me why this won't work.

Not the easy shots. I know the model is small. I know reasoning and knowledge don't cleanly separate. I know verification is hard outside code. I've spent weeks trying to kill this thing myself and it's still standing. So come at it with your best. If you can find the flaw I haven't, you've saved me months. If you can't, maybe stale-by-design was never the only way to build this.

Convince me I'm wrong.
